Privacy.

What this website and a self-hosted Comply deployment do with data.

Last updated 29 October 2026.

The short version

This marketing site sets no cookies and runs no analytics. Comply is self-hosted software: when you deploy it, the audit data stays in your environment and you decide who can reach it. The maintainers do not receive audit content from a deployment.

This website

These pages are static files. Fonts are served from this site, and there is no advertising, tracking script, or third-party embed. The host that serves the pages may keep standard access logs, such as the requested page, the time, and the network address. Those logs are controlled by the host, not by the application.

Links to GitHub leave this site. GitHub then handles the request under its own privacy terms.

A self-hosted deployment

When you run Comply, you or your organization is the controller of the data in it. That includes accounts, assessments, evidence files, conclusions, MCP keys, and the public trust center. The data lives in the directory you mount at /app/data, or in your Blob Storage when that is configured.

  • The application stores sessions in the browser's local storage, not in a cookie.
  • MCP keys are tenant-bound and can be revoked from the settings page.
  • Evidence files stay in the deployment's storage; the library refresh reads public source documents.
  • The maintainers have no access to a deployment unless you give it to them.

If you use a public trust center, the claims you publish and the header image you upload are public by design. Keep evidence behind the NDA gate.

Your choices

For a self-hosted deployment, contact the operator who runs it. For this website, open an issue in the public repository. If you believe a deployment is exposing data it should not, report it through the security advisory link on the support page.
